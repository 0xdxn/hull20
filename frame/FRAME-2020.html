UFO 2026 frame: 2020 aluminium extrusion

Same saucer: 87 in across, 36 in rim sides, hub 6⅜ in up, 4 in belly, 24 × 12 in dome on a 3¾ in riser. No T-nuts: every butt joint is one stainless M6 machine screw into the piece's tapped end hole, and the 24 octagon corners are 135° brackets through-bolted with nylock nuts. It comes apart with a 4 mm and a 3 mm hex key. Generated 2026-10-12; dimensions in mm unless marked.

No sloped joints. The top's 12.6° slope comes from the aluminet stretched rim-to-hub, and the belly's from the sail pulled down to the centre plate. So the frame is flat rings, radial spokes and vertical posts, all at 90° or 135°: stiffer than hinged joints, nothing to set by eye, cheaper.

The 135° corners (J1, J6, J7)

A regular octagon's inside corners are 135°, so each of the 24 corners (8 on the rim, 8 on the hub ring, 8 on the core ring) gets one of these brackets on its inner face. Hold the bracket in the corner, mark both holes through it, drill 5.5 mm straight through the bar with the drill guide, then bolt from the outside. If a pack comes with T-nuts and screws, they aren't used.

The bracket: a 135° 2020 corner bracket, legs 60 mm, 16 mm wide, 6 mm thick, M5 holes near the ends. 8 per pack; 3 packs for the 24 corners. (The sellers' product photos are not reproduced here.)

Cut plan (20 bars of 1220 mm, 3 mm kerf)

  • Bars 1–16: cut each to 900 (A, B) with a stop block clamped to the saw fence, so all 16 match. Each leaves a ~317 mm offcut.
  • Offcuts 1–8: cut to 300 (C hub ring), one stop-block setting for all 8.
  • Offcuts 9–16: one D 137.8 and one E 142 from each (~31 mm waste).
  • Bar 17: 5 × G 95, 1 × X 400, 2 × L 150; about 20 mm left.
  • Bar 18: 3 × G 95, 4 × F 90, 8 × K 60; about 50 mm left.
  • Bar 19: 2 × H 373 box rails (cut after the core ring is built, to its measured outside width across two opposite sides), then 1 × X 400.
  • Bar 20: 2 × X 400, 2 × L 150; about 110 mm left.

Cut each group with its own stop-block setting: matching lengths within a group matters more than the exact number. Cut the two H rails last, after the core ring is assembled, to the measured distance between its opposite inside faces (about 333 mm).

Tap the ends, with a drill: every screwed end gets an M6 × 1.0 thread, 25 mm deep, cut with the hex-shank drill-and-tap bit in a cordless drill: low speed, clutch on a low setting so it slips rather than snaps the tap, a squirt of cutting fluid, then back it out in reverse. It drills to 5.0 mm and threads in one pass, whatever size the brand's centre hole is. Clearance holes go through the crossing pieces on the drill guide: 6.5 mm for M6, 5.5 mm for M5. Machine screws in tapped holes come out and go back every season without wearing the thread.

What changes elsewhere

  • The IP67 box moves inside, onto two rails over the core ring, under the deck: hidden, out of the rain, and the feeds get shorter. Glands still face down. The wiring diagram needs this redrawn.
  • Belly LED spokes: 56 px each (448 total, was 480), in the 935 mm channels that now double as battens. The 1×2 furring battens are dropped.
  • No staples into the frame. Mesh and sail go into the slots under a 6 mm slot-cover strip, like screen spline. Try it on an offcut first; if it slips, zip-tie through the knit every 6 in.
  • Hang: four pad eyes on every other spoke (J12) are the lift points. Rigging is not covered here: if you hang one, have the structure and rigging checked by someone qualified and use rated hardware.
  • Skin: one piece top and one piece belly, cut as below; the coroplast plan and the vinyl banner template are superseded.

Snag-proofing: before any skin goes on

Aluminet and shade sail move in the wind, and a sharp aluminium edge saws through them over a month. Everything the skins touch gets softened:

  • Every cut end (62 pieces): run a deburring tool round the cut edge and the slot lips, right after cutting. Two seconds a piece.
  • The 16 outside corners on the rim and hub ring (where the square-cut ends leave a point, Fig 5): file a small round on the point, then wrap the corner in two turns of self-fusing silicone tape. The aluminet folds over the rim and drapes over the hub ring at these points.
  • Bolt tails inside the corners (48): push a vinyl cap over each nylock and bolt tail. The sail runs past the rim's inside face, right where they stick out.
  • The open wedge at each rim corner: the tape wrap covers it, so the mesh can't catch in the gap.
  • Slot cover strip in every open slot the skin touches (already on the list), so no slot lip is exposed.
  • Last check: drag a scrap of the aluminet over every face, corner and joint. Anything that catches gets filed or taped.

Build order

  1. Rim octagon on the floor (J1 × 8). Check it's flat and the across-flats is equal three ways.
  2. Core ring (J7), then the 8 spokes from rim mid-sides to the core (J2, J3). Rim and spokes flush on top.
  3. Hub posts on the spokes (J4), hub ring on the posts (J6, J5). Porthole posts (J10).
  4. Box rails (J11) and box; centre plate on its legs (J8); beam pod and iris on the plate.
  5. LED channels: lines A and B on the rim, belly battens rim → plate.
  6. Snag-proofing (above): file and tape the 16 corners, cap the 48 bolt tails, scrap-of-mesh test.
  7. Belly sail into the rim's inner slot, stapled to the plate. Aluminet into the rim's top slot, pulled up over the hub ring.
  8. Deck plate on the hub ring, clamping the mesh. Riser posts (J9), then the dome lip bolted to them.
  9. Full show on the ground, then hang.
