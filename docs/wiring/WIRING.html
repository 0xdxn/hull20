1 · System overview

2 · Controller board (perfboard inside the box)

Built on two ELPZON boards as a stack: CONTROLLER-STACK.html (hole-by-hole layout). Board build. Nano ESP32 on female headers (so it lifts out to flash), U1 and U2 in DIP-14 sockets, two-way or three-way screw terminals for J1–J6 and the 5 V input. Keep R1–R4 right at U1's outputs. Every data run leaves the board with its own GND wire beside it.
Power and flashing: the Nano ESP32 has no 5 V input pin, and its VIN needs 6–21 V (datasheet 11.3), so it takes its 5 V through its own USB-C port: a short USB-C power lead (power only) from the 5 V bus via F6. Never put 5 V on VIN. To flash, unplug that lead and plug in the laptop cable instead; nothing can backfeed.

The ESP32-S3 has four RMT transmit channels, one per data line, all sending at once. The longest line (ch 3, 689 px) sets the frame time, about 48 fps. "Worst current" is every pixel of that line full white at the 42 % cap (60 mA per pixel). The firmware's 30 A limiter holds the total; a single line can still reach its figure on its own, so each fuse is sized above it. With 16 portholes instead of 8, ch 4 becomes 358 px (10.7 ms) and F5 stays 10 A.

So: about 20 m (65 ft) of 14 AWG pair with slack, 8 m of 18 AWG pair and ≈ 20 m (65 ft) of 22/3. That's more than the 50 ft of 22/3 on the parts list, so get 100 ft. Put a 1000 µF capacitor across 5 V/GND where each strip starts (lineA, cockpit lip, S1, iris, TJ) plus C3 on the board: 6 in all. Seal every outdoor joint with adhesive-lined heat shrink.

7 · Bring-up order

  1. Bench, USB only. Controller board on USB, no supplies. Flash the test firmware. One short test strip on J1, then J2, J3, J4: each shows a single-pixel chase.
  2. Supplies, no loads. Wire the mains side, close the lid, plug into the GFCI. Meter: LRS-350-5 at 5.0 V, trim to 5.1 V. LRS-75-12 at 12 V (if fitted). Unplug.
  3. One fuse at a time, test firmware capped at 10 %: F6 (board), then F1 (lineA), F2, F3, F4, F5. On each, check the pixel count, the direction and the start corner against fig 4.
  4. Voltage at the far end. Each line full white at the 42 % cap: at least 4.6 V at the last pixel. If it's lower, add a tap from the ring bus there.
  5. Beam (if fitted). PWM ramp 0 → 100 %: the pod fades smoothly. Pull F6 and check that the pod stays dark (the pull-down works).
  6. Full show on the ground first: clamp meter on the LRS-350-5 output during an abduction, ≤ 30 A.
  7. Last. Glands tight, GFCI test button pressed, box level.

8 · Open items

1. Fog relay. Planned and not built.
2. Portholes 8 vs 16. Drawn for 8. 16 adds a second ring per top facet; ch 4 goes to 358 px, the chain doubles, nothing else changes.
3. Dome lip. Measure where the cockpit strip will actually sit before cutting the RGBW (✂10 on the strip plan): LEDs = inches × 0.79 at 60/m. 115 assumes 75.4".
4. Beam pod. Not built. Without it, leave out the LRS-75-12, F7, the MOSFET and gland G6; D6 stays unused.
