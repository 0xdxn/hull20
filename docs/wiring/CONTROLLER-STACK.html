UFO controller · wire it one connection at a time

Two boards, stacked. Board A (the big one, rows 1–30) holds the Nano ESP32 and one chip, U1. Board B (the small one) holds four resistors, and it's where the wires to the light strips and from the power box are soldered in. There are no screw terminals: every wire is soldered straight into a hole. Six short wires join the two boards.

Updated 2026-10-25 to match what's on the ship: no fog machine and no beams. Everything that stays is in the same holes as before, so anything you've already soldered is still right.

Telling the two capacitors apart

Only have up to 100 µF? Use a 100 µF can for C3 (10 V or more, stripe to row 3). This board only feeds the Nano and the chip, which draw very little, so 100 µF is plenty. Don't use the tiny ones (10 µF, 47 µF). Still not sure? Send a photo of the ones you have and I'll name each one.

No screw terminals. Every wire, including the power-box wires and the strip cables, is soldered straight into its hole.

What the marks in the drawings mean

Each coloured line in a drawing is one separate wire with exactly two ends. Nothing is spliced together. The two ends are the two holes circled in that wire's step: solder one end in each. A wire only connects where it ends in a hole.

    Naming a hole: row 17, hole A means go down to row 17 (numbers down both sides), then across to hole A (letters along the top and bottom). Board B's holes are lower-case: row 1, hole a. On board A, holes A to E of one row sit on one copper bar and are already joined; so are F to J. One row is not joined to the next, and the left half is not joined to the right half.

    The basics: power versus data, and how the copper joins holes

    1 · What runs to each light strip?

    Every strip has three wires: +5 V, GND and DATA. They come from two different places:

    • Power (+5 V and GND) comes from the power box, through its fuses, straight to the strips. It does not go through this little controller board. Nothing is 12 V any more (the beams are gone).
    • DATA comes from this controller board. Each run of lights gets one 2-wire cable soldered into board B: DATA + GND. The GND in that cable is a reference so the data signal is read cleanly; it isn't the strip's power.

    2 · How the copper on the boards works

    These are prototype boards with copper bars printed underneath. On board A, the five holes A B C D E of a row sit on one bar and are joined to each other, and so are F G H I J. The two halves are not joined across the middle, and one row is not joined to the next. The long + and − bars down each side join every hole along them. Board B works the same way, just smaller.

    The rule: you only add a wire when two things are on different bars. If two things already sit on the same bar, the copper has joined them; don't add a wire. Every step tells you exactly which two holes to join.

    Bench test before it goes in the box

    1. Chip out, Nano out. Meter board A: no beep between the + rail and the − rail.
    2. Meter board B: no beep between row 1 and row 3 on the left half (+5 V and GND).
    3. Push U1 into its socket, notch toward row 1. Push the Nano into its headers, USB-C at the row 1 end.
    4. 5 V on the power-in wires (row 1, hole a and row 3, hole a) from the power box (through F6) or a bench supply. Nano powered by its USB-C lead.
    5. Flash the test firmware (firmware/ufo-test: all four lines at once, each in its wire's colour: rim green, cockpit blue, spokes purple, portholes cyan). Connect a short test strip to one strip cable at a time (its DATA and GND wires, with the test strip's own 5 V from the same supply) and check it shows that line's colour. Passed 2026-10-25.
