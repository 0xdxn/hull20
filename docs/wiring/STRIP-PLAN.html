5 · Cuts and seals

  1. Mark before you cut. Count LEDs from the input end and cut on the copper pads between two LEDs, through the middle of the pads.
  2. The 10 cuts: ✂1 RGB 2 at 140 (end of the rim) · ✂2–✂6 RGB 3 at 56, 112, 168, 224, 280 (S1–S5) · ✂7–✂9 RGB 4 at 56, 112, 168 (S6–S8) · ✂10 RGBW at 115 (cockpit; measure the lip first, see below).
  3. 20 cut ends get sealed. Where a jumper goes on, solder first, then seal: silicone end cap filled with neutral-cure silicone, or glue-lined heat shrink over the sleeve. The spare pieces' cut ends get sealed too, so the spares stay IP67.
  4. Jumpers (22 AWG 3-core, #16): 4 at the rim (S1→S2, S3→S4, S5→S6, S7→S8), about 33" each; 3 at the hub (S2→S3, S4→S5, S6→S7), about 5" each; S8 → iris about 7". Leave 2" slack at each end.
  5. Your heat-shrink-sealed strip is best as RGB 4. Its three spokes sit in belly channels, under the saucer and out of the rain, and most of it stays on the reel as the spare.
  6. Measure the dome lip before ✂10. 115 LEDs assumes 75.4" (π × 24") at 60/m. Wrap a tape where the strip will actually sit; LEDs = inches × 0.79 (60/m). If the strip turns out to be 30/m, it's half that and the cut moves.
Spares: RGB 2 keeps 160, RGB 4 keeps 132, RGB 3 keeps 20 (312 RGB) and the RGBW keeps 185. A failed spoke or rim face can be swapped on show night. Also spare: the dense 1 m strip in its channel (wand or antenna) and a loose dense 1 m strip. Count its LEDs: 144/m is the usual dense density.
